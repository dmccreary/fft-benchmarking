# References: Capstone: Applications, Design, and Reporting

1. [Design of experiments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Design_of_experiments) - Wikipedia - Explains research questions, independent and dependent variables, and controlled conditions as the structural components of a rigorous experiment, the framework this chapter asks every capstone project to follow when scoping and testing.

2. [Software-defined radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software-defined_radio) - Wikipedia - Describes how demodulation and filtering move from dedicated analog hardware into general-purpose software, the architecture behind this chapter's software-defined-radio capstone domain and its FFT-based spectrum analysis.

3. [Condition monitoring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condition_monitoring) - Wikipedia - Covers vibration analysis for rotating machinery, explaining how specific frequency signatures reveal bearing wear before failure, the exact machine-monitoring application this chapter connects back to Chapter 15's peak-detection technique.

4. Understanding Digital Signal Processing (3rd Edition) - Richard G. Lyons - Prentice Hall - Lyons is best known for his exceptionally clear "quadrature signals" tutorial bridging complex-number math to real receiver hardware, making his applied audio, radar, and communications chapters an accessible bridge into this chapter's capstone domains.

5. Writing for Computer Science (3rd Edition) - Justin Zobel - Springer - Zobel's guidance on stating a precise, answerable research question and structuring an honest methodology and limitations section is widely assigned in CS research-methods courses, directly matching this chapter's report-writing requirements.

6. [Orthogonal Frequency-Division Multiplexing (OFDM) Explained](https://www.mathworks.com/discovery/ofdm.html) - MathWorks - Explains how an IFFT converts frequency-domain data into a transmittable time-domain OFDM symbol and an FFT recovers it at the receiver, the FFT mechanism underlying this chapter's communication-systems application domain.

7. [FPGA Implementation of an Efficient FFT Processor for FMCW Radar Signal Processing](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8512539/) - PMC (National Library of Medicine) - Peer-reviewed paper detailing how range and Doppler FFTs extract a target's distance and velocity from a radar's reflected chirp signal, a worked example of this chapter's radar-processing capstone domain.

8. [Limitations of the Study](https://libguides.usc.edu/writingguide/limitations) - USC Libraries Research Writing Guide - Explains how to identify, describe, and justify a study's limitations, and argues that stating them plainly strengthens rather than weakens a report, the exact standard this chapter's limitations-statement section teaches.

9. [Independent and Dependent Variables](https://libguides.usc.edu/writingguide/variables) - USC Libraries Research Writing Guide - Defines independent and dependent variables with a practical sentence-completion technique for identifying each, directly supporting this chapter's experimental-design worked example around window choice and frequency accuracy.

10. [A Quick Guide to Writing a Solid Peer Review](https://eos.org/science-updates/a-quick-guide-to-writing-a-solid-peer-review) - Eos (American Geophysical Union) - Outlines what a careful peer reviewer checks for, including whether conclusions are actually supported by the data, the same critical read this chapter asks a classmate to give a capstone report before it is finished.
